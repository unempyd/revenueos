# Evidence and guardrails

Use this note to anchor claims made while applying the skill. Recheck the live pages when current wording or guidance matters.

## Source basis

### Koray Tuğberk Gübür's framework

Koray's 2022 Oncrawl case study describes a quality threshold for a query or query network, a range formed by cornerstone sources, and different thresholds for different source or website types. It also connects performance to source-level relevance, topical authority, factual consistency, information structure, and a material change from the source's prior state.

Source: [The Importance of Quality Thresholds and Predictive Ranking](https://www.oncrawl.com/technical-seo/importance-quality-thresholds-predictive-ranking/)

Treat this as Koray's comparative model and case-study interpretation. It is not confirmation that Google exposes or calculates one public metric with this name. The case study cannot isolate content quality from authority, links, history, reputation, freshness, or other ranking systems.

Use the correct spelling **Koray Tuğberk Gübür** in prose. Preserve a publisher's capitalization only inside a verbatim title or byline.

### Google's people-first content guidance

Google's self-assessment questions ask whether content supplies original information or analysis, substantial and complete coverage, additional value rather than copying, trustworthy sourcing and expertise, and substantial value compared with other search results. Google also advises evaluating the overall page experience rather than focusing on one or two aspects.

Source: [Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)

Use this guidance to support the five audit gates. Do not turn its questions into claimed direct ranking weights.

### Search Quality Evaluator Guidelines

Google's evaluator framework distinguishes Page Quality from Needs Met. The guidelines help raters evaluate search-system output; rater scores do not directly determine a page's ranking.

Sources:

- [Search Quality Evaluator Guidelines](https://guidelines.raterhub.com/searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf)
- [Google's explanation of the guidelines and rater role](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/11/search-quality-rater-guidelines-update)

Use the distinction to prevent a common error: a reputable, high-quality page may still be a poor result for a particular query, and a highly relevant page may still have weak quality or trust.

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer, in order:

1. Direct observation of the current SERP and target/comparator pages
2. Primary datasets, methods, official documentation, and first-party evidence
3. Corroborated specialist reporting or analysis
4. Search snippets and third-party summaries only as leads

Use third-party SEO metrics only for the dimension they estimate. Domain authority, traffic estimates, and link counts do not measure the content threshold by themselves.

## Claim labels

Use these distinctions in analysis:

- **Observed:** directly visible in the cited SERP or page
- **Inferred:** best explanation supported by several observations
- **Unknown:** not verifiable with available access
- **Recommendation:** proposed action, not a fact about Google's systems

Avoid causal verbs such as "ranks because" unless controlled evidence exists. Prefer "is consistent with," "may be supported by," or "the comparison suggests."

## Non-universal tactics

Do not require any of the following without task-specific evidence:

- Preserving image EXIF data
- Producing unique n-grams
- Using Q&A structure on every page
- Refreshing an arbitrary percentage of a site
- Reducing links as a general quality tactic
- Adding FAQ schema to raise quality
- Maximizing entity or fact counts regardless of readability
- Changing dates without substantive updates

Evaluate the overall result: intent satisfaction, information gain, evidence, source qualification, trust, and retrieval experience.
